## Abstract

Predicting cellular responses to perturbations is complicated by unpaired measurements and incomplete regulatory information. We developed Epi-C3, a research prototype combining count-aware representation learning, conditional latent transport, and optional epigenomic features and candidate chromatin-contact graphs. In an internal genome-wide K562 CRISPRi evaluation, four RNA-only and epigenomic model arms sharing one representation all selected zero perturbation strength on validation. Their target-excluded mean-expression error on 347 held-out targets therefore matched an exact-input no-effect baseline (0.016045), whereas a training-mean baseline achieved 0.015711. A post-hoc, single-seed GEARS extension with an additional public GO prior achieved 0.015831; it was not a blinded, common-prior comparison. The shuffled graph changed only 31.25% of edges, and only two graph-source targets were held out, limiting inference about topology. In a separate post-hoc validation analysis, a target-independent count-transport candidate improved macro-MSE over no effect but not over the log-expression training-mean baseline. The epigenomic inputs were unpaired bulk measurements, and candidate links do not establish causal regulation. These findings define a prototype and a negative internal result, not evidence of epigenomic benefit. Prospective, independent comparisons and biological replication are required.

## Introduction

Single-cell perturbation experiments observe populations before and after intervention rather than the same cell in both states. Models must therefore infer counterfactual distributions while accounting for cell state, intervention identity, assay context, and unpaired sampling. Recent systematic benchmarking has highlighted discrepancies between accuracy on absolute expression, perturbation effects, and differential-expression recovery [1].

External biological knowledge may constrain predictions when perturbation data are limited. GeneCompass illustrates knowledge-informed representation learning [2], and knowledge-driven drug models offer another approach to predictions for unprofiled interventions [3]. Chromatin accessibility, transcription-factor binding, DNA methylation, and three-dimensional contacts could inform context-dependent candidate regulatory networks. However, bulk assays from different sources do not constitute paired measurements of the same single cells, and a genomic contact does not prove a regulatory effect.

We designed Epi-C3 to integrate candidate regulatory priors with count-aware representations and conditional latent transport. Here we describe the prototype and several preliminary evaluations. The pending confirmatory work includes independent splits, no-prior and shuffled-prior controls, matched simple baselines, and applicable reference models evaluated under common conditions.

## Results

### Representation, transport, and optional chromatin priors

The prototype accepts count matrices and feature-availability masks. A count-aware encoder uses log-normalized expression and visibility masks; masked views support a teacher–student representation objective. A negative-binomial decoder, regularization terms, and a conditional latent velocity network model cell states and population-level perturbation responses. The genetic-intervention path can incorporate epigenomic features and a sparse candidate regulatory graph; the drug path uses molecular information and dose (Figure 1).

Candidate HCT116 features include available accessibility, selected transcription-factor peaks, called chromatin contacts, and promoter or linked-element methylation with explicit provenance and coverage masks. These data are not paired with the single cells used for training. After validation of the hg38 reference, the HCT116 WGBS processing retained 53,332,109 measurement rows. The resulting 4,096-gene bundle marked promoter methylation as available for 4,081 genes and linked-element methylation for 2,390; it records 44,219 candidate contact loops and 3,913 gene-graph edges. These are candidate associations, not causal regulatory effects, and availability does not imply genome-wide measurement at every locus.

A model using the full-genome prior was trained on 35,309 supervised HCT116 cells with 12,000 bounded reference cells; within-training calibration selected effect strength zero. A separate three-arm transfer from the full Tabula representation compared no prior, the real prior, and a topology-only shuffled graph. At fixed effect strength one, local PDS cosine values were 0.4511, 0.4728, and 0.4764, respectively; the no-effect reference was 0.4493. These are descriptive, uncalibrated values on a previously examined holdout, and the real prior did not exceed the shuffled control on this metric.

An independent audit found nonpositive noise-corrected expression-distance denominators for 13 of the 24 held-out interventions. The evaluator's pooled ratio of sums is still mathematically defined because its pooled denominator is positive, but the weak individual denominators limit its interpretability. We therefore do not use that pooled normalized-expression metric to rank the arms or claim a prior benefit.

### Preliminary KHP CD8 T-cell transfer comparison

The KHP PBMC dataset distributed with the benchmark authors' resources [1,4] contains 127,595 cells on a 5,000-gene axis. We froze a common split for ten interventions. For each intervention, model training used 400 control and 400 treated cells from each of the B- and CD4 T-cell source populations, together with 400 target CD8 T-cell input controls. All CD8 T-cell treatment cells were withheld. An additional 400 nonoverlapping CD8 T-cell reference controls and 21,673 target-treatment cells across the ten interventions were used for evaluation.

We compared exact-input no effect, an equally weighted additive mean effect from the two source cell types, a condition-specific RNA-only C3 model trained from scratch, and scGen 2.1.0 [5]. ScGen used source-cell latent arithmetic; negative decoded log-expression values were projected to zero. All methods used the same target input cells, evaluation references, and gene axis.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| No effect | 0.017461 | 0.172245 | 0.006224 | 0.068827 |
| Source mean additive | 0.006194 | 0.771672 | 0.009700 | 0.075030 |
| C3 RNA-only | 0.012311 | 0.466169 | 0.005421 | 0.063752 |
| scGen | 0.007829 | 0.632018 | 0.079746 | 0.180598 |

After examining the original four-method results, we added an explicitly post-hoc scVIDR extension [8]. It uses a two-source latent-regression adapter, not the unmodified upstream prediction workflow, and was run in a separately adapted CPU environment. Its ten-condition mean expression MSE was 0.009871, delta Pearson 0.591462, per-gene RBF MMD² 0.076248, and Wasserstein distance 0.177621. Independent code recomputed MSE and delta Pearson for this extension; distributional metrics were checked for file integrity but not independently recomputed. The post-hoc addition should not be presented as a prespecified five-method comparison.

These are descriptive ten-condition averages on `log1p(CPM 10,000)` recomputed over the selected genes. Reference-centered delta Pearson subtracts the separate reference-control mean from both predicted and treated means; the exact-input no-effect baseline can therefore have nonzero correlation when input and reference controls differ. C3 had lower mean-expression error than no effect for seven of ten conditions; the source-mean and scGen arms did so for all ten. The C3 mean-expression error was higher than those of the source-mean and scGen arms, while its mean per-gene MMD² was lower. These metrics assess different properties and do not establish an overall winner. Significant-DE top-50 overlap was defined for different subsets of conditions across methods, so the corresponding subset averages are not directly comparable.

The target cell type's controls were available during fitting and inference; thus, this is not a completely unseen-cell-type experiment. The source 5,000-gene selection may have used held-out cells, appropriate donor and assay-batch metadata were unavailable for matching, and controls were reused across interventions. Some interventions were peptides or proteins rather than small molecules. This non-blind single-dataset experiment does not test the epigenomic prior.

### Internal held-out single-gene pilot

We used the stimulated subset of the scPerturb Datlinger 2021 Jurkat dataset [6]. The perturbation background included fixed TCR stimulation; each target had two original CRISPR guides. We froze a 12/4/4 training/validation/test gene split and selected 2,000 features using training cells, with all target identities forced into the feature axis. A C3 effect strength of 0.5 was selected using only the four validation genes. On four held-out test genes, mean-expression MSE was 0.010305 for no effect, 0.011115 for the training-mean baseline, 0.010313 for C3 RNA-only, and 0.014716 for an adapted GEARS model [7]. The GEARS adapter uses public GO annotations and an expanded per-cell coexpression graph; 47.5% of its raw log-expression predictions were projected to zero. This is not an unmodified upstream GEARS result. Treatment and control cells came from different wells, controls were reused across targets, and these four genes do not provide independent biological replication.

### Post-hoc K562 multimodal graph ablation

We assembled an exploratory K562 graph from unpaired bulk assays, including accessible chromatin, chromatin-contact evidence, DNA methylation, and transcription-factor binding. Seven training-axis target factors had qualifying K562 ChIP-seq tracks. The 4,238-edge candidate graph contained 44 promoter-contact edges and 4,194 additional TF-derived edges; the edges are proposed associations, not validated causal regulation. A degree- and weight-preserving shuffle changed 1,299 edges (30.65%), limiting the strength of the structural control. The graph directly covered 11 of 124 training conditions; direct coverage in the test set was 2/4, 6/28, and 3/12 conditions for seen0, seen1, and seen2, respectively.

After the original test outcomes had been examined, we trained five new arms using one shared frozen RNA representation and three matched flow seeds per arm. Every arm retained RNA target identity. The arms added, respectively, nothing, epigenomic node features without a graph, a real graph without epigenomic node features, epigenomic node features plus the real graph, or the same node features plus the shuffled graph. The three seeds vary flow initialization and sampling but are neither independent representation pretraining runs nor biological replicates. At a common effect strength of 1.0, class-wise MSEs were:

### Post-hoc K562 topology stress test

The original K562 shuffle changed 1,299/4,238 edges (30.65%). Longer degree- and weight-preserving swap chains did not establish mixing and continued to change only approximately 30–32% of edges. A separate layer-wise relaxed network-flow calculation gave an upper bound of 2,269/4,238 (53.54%) changed edges. Because the relaxed layers may reuse the same directed pair across weights, this is an upper bound, not a demonstrated feasible graph or a random sample.

We then constructed an optimized structural stress graph by fixing all 319 weight-two edges and maximizing changed edges in the weight-one layer while preserving each node's weighted-layer in- and out-degrees, prohibiting self-loops and duplicates. It changed 2,063/4,238 edges (48.68%). The EGR1 source neighborhood changed only 18.6%; other source neighborhoods changed more. This graph is an optimized, post-hoc stress test, **not an unbiased random null or a significance test**. Synthetic edge labels encode structural weight, not biological evidence.

Three stress-graph flow models were trained using the same node features, frozen representation, initialization per seed, batch sequence, and random streams as the real-graph comparators; each was trained for 1,500 updates. At common effect strength 1.0, test mean-expression MSEs were:

| Graph arm | seen0 (n=4) | seen1 (n=28) | seen2 (n=12) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Real | 0.052206 | 0.045513 | 0.043255 |
| Original shuffle | 0.052141 | 0.045534 | 0.043223 |
| Optimized stress | 0.052188 | 0.045550 | 0.043252 |

Across 44 test combinations and three flow seeds, stress-minus-real MSE averaged +0.00002123; in the 11 directly covered combinations it averaged +0.00014009, and in the remaining 33 it averaged -0.00001839. In validation, the corresponding overall difference was -0.00001448. Directions varied by seed and partition, and the numerical differences were small. Three seeds share one representation and do not provide biological replication; no statistical or causal benefit is established. The comparison uses a previously examined test split and unpaired bulk assays.

### Internal iPSC-neuron CRISPRi held-out-target test

We independently trained an RNA-only C3 model from scratch on the Tian iPSC-induced neuron CRISPRi dataset in scPerturb [6]. The frozen within-dataset split used 109, 36, and 36 mapped training, validation, and test targets; three source target names absent from the gene axis were excluded rather than assigned speculative aliases. A 2,000-gene axis was chosen using 19,337 training cells, including all 181 known mapped target identities. The 260 training controls and separate validation/test input and reference control pools were divided within each technical batch. For each held-out target, input and reference controls were sampled separately to approximate the treated-cell batch proportions, using at most 40 cells per role; controls could recur across targets.

Validation selected C3 effect strength zero from five candidate strengths before test reference preparation. On 36 test targets, mean-expression MSE was 0.019180 for no effect, 0.024844 for the training-mean baseline, and 0.019180 for C3. C3's zero-strength predictions were exactly identical to the no-effect predictions; this is not evidence that the model predicted perturbation-specific changes. Independent code recomputed validation selection, both simple baselines, and test MSE and delta Pearson. DE, Spearman, and distribution metrics were not independently recomputed. The available cell-level top-20 DE overlap was undefined for all 36 targets for each of the three methods.

For the protocol-specified reference-centered delta calculation, even no-effect predictions obtained a mean Pearson correlation of 0.574. The same reference-control mean is subtracted from both the observed and predicted means, so differences between input and reference control pools can contribute to this correlation. In a post-hoc sensitivity analysis, predicted effects were instead centered on each prediction's actual input controls; the no-effect and C3 predicted effect vectors were exactly zero and their Pearson correlations were undefined. The training-mean input-centered correlation averaged -0.024. This diagnostic is not a prespecified primary metric and does not establish a causal explanation for every observed correlation.

This is a new model trained within the Tian dataset, not an external blind test of the same pretrained model used elsewhere. The source values are nonnegative and integer-like, but raw-count provenance is not independently established. Batches are not established biological replicates, the comparison uses one seed, and no matched epigenomic prior or additional reference model was included. The results are not official challenge scores.

### Internal Replogle K562 CRISPRi held-out-target test

We trained a new RNA-only C3 model on the scPerturb release of the Replogle K562 essential-gene CRISPRi library [6,11]. Of the 8,563 source genes, 1,864 overlapped a 2,000-gene axis previously selected using Norman training cells; this was a post-hoc cross-study feature choice. A fixed hash split assigned 635 eligible perturbation targets to 381 training, 127 validation, and 127 test targets. ## Methods

### Data and provenance

The framework uses public single-cell reference and perturbation resources, including Arc, Tahoe-100M, X-Atlas-Orion, and a CELLxGENE-curated Tabula Sapiens release. The curated Tabula source has not been established as byte-identical to the Figshare distribution. Dataset snapshots, sample-selection rules, feature axes, input checksums, and preprocessing versions must be included in the final data-availability supplement. Raw human single-cell data will not be redistributed in the code repository without a separate access and license review.

For epigenomic integration, HCT116-matched ENCODE chromatin measurements and public whole-genome bisulfite data are mapped to a common gene axis with explicit feature-availability masks. Candidate promoter, transcription-factor, and chromatin-contact associations are assembled from the available evidence. A downloaded HFFc6 4DN Hi-C object is a different cellular context and is not treated as HCT116- or K562-matched evidence; its file metadata record genome assembly as unknown, requiring further provenance before coordinate integration.

### KHP training and evaluation

Counts were normalized within each cell over the author's 5,000-gene axis and transformed with `log1p`. The no-effect baseline copied the target input controls. The source-mean baseline added the equal-weight average of source-cell treated-minus-control mean effects, followed by nonnegative projection. For each condition, C3 learned a representation and latent transport from the allowed cells without an epigenomic prior or external pretraining. ScGen was trained separately for each condition using the shared allowed observations and source-cell latent arithmetic.

For each intervention, absolute mean-expression and treated-minus-reference-control effect metrics were calculated on all available cells. Cell-level Wilcoxon rank-sum testing and Benjamini–Hochberg correction were used for exploratory differential-expression summaries; they are not biological-replicate inference. Significant-DE overlap was left undefined when either relevant list had fewer than the required number of genes. Per-gene biased RBF MMD² used gamma 1.0, and Wasserstein distances used equal samples of at most 100 predicted and reference cells per condition; this differs from the paper's scripts, which commonly use up to 500 cells. The evaluation protocol was frozen before reference scoring, but the wider study is not blinded. Undefined metrics were retained as missing rather than filled with zero.

### Single-gene and combination pilot methods

In the Datlinger pilot, the training-only gene axis and control splits were frozen before validation or test scoring; the four test targets were held out from training. Predictions shared input controls and the same selected-axis `log1p(CPM 10,000)` representation. The adapted GEARS model uses public GOA, training-control coexpression, and an explicitly expanded batch graph. The C3 and training-mean approaches did not use this external GO prior. All GEARS and baseline negative normalized-expression values were projected to zero.

For the Norman combination pilot, 86 training single-target conditions and 38 training combinations were used along with training controls, yielding 62,113 training cells. A 2,000-gene axis was selected on training cells, forcing 102 known target identities into the axis. We used 44 validation and 44 test combinations. Validation compared both C3 variants at five predeclared effect strengths using equally weighted seen-class average mean-expression MSE. The selected model and strength were frozen before test-reference preparation. The no-effect and additive-single-target baselines shared each case's matched input controls. The additive baseline used only training single-target effects, separately by gemgroup, and assigned zero effect to unseen targets. Independent code recomputed the validation selection and all test MSE and delta-Pearson means; DE and distribution metrics were not independently recomputed. After the original test scores had been observed, GEARS was trained for 8,000 updates on the frozen training set using public GO annotations, a training-control coexpression graph, explicit graph batching, and fixed parameters; its test predictions used the same sampled input controls and selected gene axis. This is an adapted implementation and its external information differs from the original C3 arm.

### Post-hoc K562 graph construction and matched-flow ablation

The K562 candidate graph retained the 44 original directed promoter-contact edges. Added TF-to-gene promoter evidence required a shared genomic base among a TF ChIP-seq peak, the gene promoter, and an ATAC-seq peak; contact-linked evidence required a shared base between a TF peak and an ATAC-overlapping opposite Hi-C anchor linked to the gene. Evidence-type counts supplied nonnegative structural weights, not estimated causal effect sizes. Bulk K562 tracks were not paired to the Norman single cells. Two WGBS files labeled as biological replicates do not establish independent donors; methylation-availability masks indicate at least one qualifying CpG in a region, not complete regional coverage. The real and shuffled graphs used identical epigenomic node features, and evidence labels attached to shuffled edges were synthetic structural-weight labels rather than biological support.

Each arm copied the same frozen RNA representation, reinitialized its flow network by seed, and trained for 1,500 steps with batch size 64. Within each seed, the initial flow weights and sampled training sequence were matched across arms. For graph arms, the target embedding used separately normalized identity and neighbor vectors, mixed with weight 0.5 and renormalized; targets without neighbors retained their identity embedding. The epigenomic adapter was trained only in the relevant arms. The primary descriptive topology contrast used a common fixed strength of 1.0; validation-selected strengths were reported separately. Both plans were frozen after earlier tests were observed and therefore are post-hoc analyses.

### Norman direct-count local diagnostic

Fifteen fixed-strength predictions, comprising five arms by three flow seeds, were replayed deterministically and their sampled integer counts were retained before normalization. Re-normalizing each replay exactly reproduced its stored prediction. The count-generation procedure multiplies each input count by a predicted fold change before residual rounding, so it cannot produce a positive count where the input count was zero. This is a material limitation for modeling gene activation and count distributions. The exact-input no-effect baseline retained the original input counts. The local diagnostic's 44 test combinations used 1,420 pooled K562 reference-control cells; input and reference identities were checked to be disjoint, although controls could be reused across interventions. All 44 noise-corrected expression-distance denominators were positive in this particular adaptation. The normalized expression metric was checked as the ratio of the mean capped unbiased MSE to the mean unbiased reference-effect distance; it is an aggregate-only metric, not a mean of per-condition ratios. Differential-expression calculations use cells rather than independent biological replicates.

For the validation-only zero-support experiment, the alternative formed a count mean by adding the input library size times the decoder's post-transport minus pre-transport probability vector to the input counts, scaled by a candidate effect strength. Negative values were projected to zero and the remaining mean was renormalized to the input library size; residual rounding then generated integer samples. The alternative used the same frozen latent transport and graph conditioner as the original model and reproduced the input exactly at strength zero. Five strengths per decoder were evaluated, and a single decoder and strength were chosen by averaging MSE within each seen class, averaging the classes equally, and then averaging equally across all five arms and three flow seeds. This post-hoc validation selection was performed after earlier test results and other validation analyses had been observed.

### Post-hoc count-space transport

For each sampled validation input control, the candidate applied its training-batch average log-CPM shift at a fixed strength in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}. After projection to nonnegative values, it renormalized the selected-axis mean to the input cell's selected-axis total and used systematic fixed-total rounding. The method exactly returns the input at strength zero and preserves the selected-axis total for every sampled row. Candidate predictions used training data and validation input controls, not validation treatment or reference expression; validation responses were used for scoring and five-fold target-level strength selection. Target exclusion occurred after full-axis normalization, as in the primary objective.

Independent code recomputed 1,735 candidate and 694 baseline per-target MSEs, with a maximum absolute difference of 2.78×10⁻¹⁷, and replayed a small prespecified set of cases. The cross-fitted folds share controls and batches and do not constitute independent donors or biological repeats.

### Topology stress, count scoring, and validation blend

Long constrained swap trajectories preserved directed degree and weight strata but do not prove uniform mixing. A relaxed linear-program calculation optimized each weight layer independently; permitting cross-layer pair reuse makes the derived maximum-change value an upper bound. The stress graph instead held weight-two edges fixed and optimized the weight-one layer subject to joint simple-graph constraints. Real, shuffle, and stress models used the same target-identity-preserving residual conditioner, identical epigenomic nodes, matched flow seeds and training streams, and a common test effect strength of 1.0. Validation and test MSE and two centered Pearson measures were independently recalculated; the graph controls are structural comparisons rather than causal tests.

The local count adapter used the same frozen reference, condition encoding, and controls across graph arms. It retained direct residual-rounding outputs rather than converting normalized predictions into pseudo-counts. The 44 combination identities were encoded as synthetic labels with target removal disabled. Per-condition evaluator results were aggregated independently, including a ratio of sums for pooled normalized expression error; uncertainty intervals and official ranking claims were not derived.

Decoder comparisons used one shared decoder and effect strength across the five original arms and three flow seeds, with equal weighting of exposure strata. The additive and masked-support alternatives were validation-only; masked support had a fixed exploratory weight of 0.1. The subsequent training-singles/C3 interpolation operates on mean `log1p(CPM 10,000)` expression, and its common weight was selected from 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1 on the repeatedly used validation partition. Neither selection was a blinded external evaluation.

### Target-exclusion sensitivity methods

For PDS, we computed count pseudobulks on the original selected gene axis using the local `vcc2026` bulk-log normalization, then removed the union of the two targets for every combination from both prediction and reference effect vectors. Cosine distances were ranked using the preset's panel-wide comparison, midrank ties, real-control source, and `n-1` denominator. We first reproduced the original all-gene PDS per condition and independently recalculated the masked and unmasked scores from grouped count sums. The evaluator's one-gene-per-label target map was not changed.

For descriptive MSE, prediction and treated-reference means were calculated after `log1p(CPM 10,000)` normalization on the full 2,000-gene axis; the per-condition squared error was then averaged over the 1,998 genes other than that condition's two targets. A second sensitivity removed the full 46-gene target panel. Full-axis MSE was checked against earlier frozen scores, and target-excluded MSE was independently recomputed by subtracting target residual sums, with reference normalization calculated separately in float64. These are sensitivity analyses on previously examined data, not official challenge scores.

### Dual-target DE and pooled-expression sensitivity methods

Using `cell-eval2` 0.22.0, `pdex` 0.3.0, and the local `vcc2026` preset, we first reproduced the previously recorded full-axis per-condition DE scores. Counts were normalized to per-cell CPM 1,000,000 for DE, using the common real reference controls; the 5 CPM reference-only gene filter and each condition's BH-adjusted p-values were calculated before target removal. For every synthetic combination, one target was excluded through the evaluator's native single-target mechanism and the other was removed from both saved DE tables after adjustment. We verified identical scores when reversing the two targets and when removing both rows before calling the native metrics. This is bookkeeping for the evaluator's single-target interface, not a claim that the combinations have no biological targets.

For expression, count pseudobulk sums were scaled to 50,000 and `log1p` transformed over the complete selected axis. Per condition, both target squared-difference coordinates were subsequently removed from the prediction error and reference-effect distance, and sums were divided by 1,998 retained genes. The native scalar delete-1 jackknife corrections remain computed over all 2,000 genes; they were not relabeled as restricted-axis corrections. The prediction correction was capped against the reference correction and the across-condition retained-coordinate budget. We required the zero-exclusion and one-target implementations to match the unmodified evaluator per condition before interpreting the two-target extension.

An independent implementation calculated the DE scoring formulas from the saved real and predicted DE tables, and separately recomputed group-sum pseudobulks, delete-1 jackknife corrections, the cross-condition budget, expression numerators, denominators, and their pooled ratio directly from frozen count matrices. Per-condition BH adjustments were independently reapplied to the saved p-values. A subsequent post-hoc audit selected the same 32 of 1,981 reference-filtered features for every condition by sorting a fixed, domain-separated SHA-256 hash of each gene name. It reconstructed per-cell CPM on the full 2,000-gene count axis, applied `log1p`, and checked the selected normalized values bitwise against the evaluator. SciPy 1.18.1 then independently recalculated two-sided asymptotic Mann–Whitney U p-values with tie and continuity corrections for all 44 real and 19-by-44 predicted condition groups against the 1,420 pooled real controls. The 28,160 comparisons agreed within relative tolerance 1e-8 plus absolute tolerance 1e-300; the remaining 1,949 filtered genes per condition were not independently retested. In a synthetic fully tied case SciPy returned an undefined p-value while the existing backend returned 1; the audit rejects undefined results rather than silently substituting a value, and no such undefined case occurred in this sample. Neither audit provides independent biological replication or certifies the official challenge rules.

### Spear-ATAC promoter-accessibility methods

The scPerturb ATAC archive [10] was analyzed with its exact nonempty `perturbation` labels. The study reports hg38, but the assembly and coordinate convention of this processed archive were not independently established. In the public scPerturb processing code at commit `b69f72a070a92bcbaf41e7f9897b11598109ab48`, `guide` maps from `sgAssign` and `perturbation` from `sgAssignFinal`; the published script applies sgRNA count >20 and specificity >0.8 but leaves an accessibility-purity filter commented out. We do not infer missing perturbation assignments. The processing code builds the peak set using perturbation groups and does not explicitly set the ArchR peak-matrix ceiling; the exact ArchR version and whether this archive was capped are not established. Therefore, we refer to values as processed peak-matrix counts, not validated raw insertion or fragment counts.

Promoter peaks were selected by the archive's `peakType=Promoter` and exact `nearestGene` symbol match to the CEBPB target neighborhoods of the three preexisting candidate graphs. For each sample and contrast, case and reference values were summed across the respective labeled guide groups. For each gene we computed `log2((case promoter sum + 0.5)/(case all-peak sum + 1)) - log2((reference promoter sum + 0.5)/(reference all-peak sum + 1))`, averaged the six sample-specific ratios equally, took the absolute value, and averaged over each target set. The shared post-hoc eligibility mask required at least 10 processed promoter counts in each sample's fixed six-guide reference group: 725 of the 852 annotated graph-union genes met the criterion. The primary CEBPB contrast used the 1,408 labeled NT cells remaining after excluding `sgsgNT-5`, `-6`, `-8`, `-11`, and `-12`; all 1,929 NT cells were also analyzed. A comparison against the same six-guide, 993-cell reference used by the placebo was retained. The single placebo used 415 cells from `sgsgNT-1`, `-10`, and `-13` against those same 993 reference cells. No confidence intervals, p-values, or biological-replicate inference were calculated.

One streaming extraction read all 242,919,341 declared nonzero peak-matrix entries, checked ordering and values, verified every cell and peak sum and nonzero count against archive metadata, and read the nested compressed member to EOF. Independent code reread raw matrix entries for 16 hash-selected genes whose annotated peaks were wholly within the first 25,000 matrix columns: 33 peaks and 1,632 sample-guide-gene values matched the extraction; all 102 group cell and library totals were also reconstructed from metadata. The independent audit recomputed 34,368 numeric scores from the extracted summaries within absolute tolerance 1e-12 (maximum difference 7.1e-15). The raw-matrix recheck is restricted to that early-column sample and is not an independent full-matrix replication. Neither this calculation nor its audit establishes causal regulation or prospective model performance.

### Preliminary Tahoe diagnostic methods

For each plate-matched condition, each cell was normalized on the complete 62,710-gene axis to 10,000 and `log1p` transformed. Per-gene cell means were used to calculate mean-expression MSE; delta Pearson compared predicted and treated mean effects after subtracting the same plate-matched reference-control mean. The no-effect and conditional-flow arms used the same sampled input controls.

An independent implementation re-read the saved reference, input-control, null, flow, and no-effect matrices, checked matching gene axes and control identities, and recomputed five per-condition values from sparse rows. It verified that the two no-effect outputs matched their selected inputs exactly. The reanalysis uses the same previously examined samples and predictions and does not independently retrain the model or establish biological replication.

### Planned comparative evaluation

The planned evaluation follows the task families in [1]: unseen single-gene perturbations, combination perturbations stratified by prior exposure to their constituent genes, and cross-cell-type transfer. Applicable models must share frozen splits, gene axes, controls, sampling, and permitted information, with pretraining exposure and target-control availability reported. Effect-size stratification and uncertainty should be assessed at the intervention or biological-replicate level where supported. The paper's ranking and missing-value handling must be distinguished from sensitivity analyses. Model-specific limitations and unavailable metrics will be reported rather than replaced by incomparable published scores.

## Discussion

The preliminary KHP results show that a simple source-mean baseline and scGen can outperform the RNA-only C3 arm on average mean-expression error, while distributional metrics need not follow the same ordering. The Tahoe analysis likewise shows that a conditional model can underperform a no-effect baseline. These observations motivate careful separation of expression accuracy, perturbation-specific signal, distributional quality, and robustness.

The Tian sensitivity analysis also illustrates that reference-centered delta Pearson can be nonzero for an exact-input no-effect prediction. Shared reference subtraction and input/reference control imbalance complicate effect-correlation interpretation across these experiments; their biological meaning is not established by numerical score recomputation alone. The Replogle RNA-only internal split likewise selected no effect and favors a training-mean baseline on the primary MSE; this is a negative result for perturbation-specific C3 performance on that protocol, not an epigenomic-prior test. In the separate genome-wide K562 split, each epigenomic and RNA arm also selected zero strength and matched no effect, while the training mean had lower macro-MSE. Because the selected graph arms make the same no-effect prediction and only two graph-source targets were held out, these results neither demonstrate a graph advantage nor establish equivalence of nonzero graph models.

The post-hoc genome-wide GEARS extension modestly reduced mean-expression MSE relative to no effect, but the training-mean baseline remained better. Its external GO prior, known held-out target identities, single seed, reused controls, and previously observed test outcomes prevent a matched-prior or prospective generalization claim.

The post-hoc target-independent count-transport candidate can activate input-zero entries, but its validation macro-MSE remained worse than the log-expression training-mean baseline. Its fixed-total rounding and newly nonzero outputs are not biological validation. The challenge-style audit shows that adapting normalized outputs to a count-based evaluation interface can alter methods unequally. Directly retaining Norman model count samples avoids that particular reconstruction step, but does not validate their biological count distribution, the source-count provenance, or equivalence to the official challenge protocol. Comparisons requiring measured-count semantics need natively compatible outputs or a separately validated common-domain evaluation; deterministic pseudo-counts alone do not solve the mismatch. Independent studies, matched biological replicates where available, and additional baselines are needed before any generalization or epigenomic-benefit claim.

The new internal pilots reinforce the need for strong simple baselines: the selected C3 model did not improve mean-expression error over no effect in the four-gene Datlinger test and did not outperform the additive training-single baseline in Norman seen1 or seen2. They do not support claims of superiority, causal chromatin effects, or robustness across biological replicates. Additional methods, datasets, seeds, and independent validation remain necessary.

Excluding panel targets from local PDS and each combination's own targets from descriptive MSE did not resolve the limitations of pooled controls, unpaired assays, post-hoc decisions, or incomplete model comparison. The training-singles baseline remained stronger in seen1 and seen2.

The separate post-hoc Spear-ATAC analysis provides an additional description of accessibility for one K562 perturbation, not an independent validation of the RNA model. Different graph target sets, a perturbation-informed source peak set, and one non-random placebo prevent a causal or statistical topology claim.

The current framework is a prototype, not a validated complete virtual cell. Its candidate graphs are derived from bulk and incompletely matched assays; the reported KHP and Tahoe comparisons do not use them. The completed full-genome HCT116 analysis reused a previously examined holdout, while the K562 real-versus-shuffled difference was negligible under a weak shuffle and sparse direct coverage. The later optimized stress graph changed more edges but is not a random null; expression and local count-based scores gave small, mixed differences. Both datasets need independent replication with matched controls and biologically appropriate data.
